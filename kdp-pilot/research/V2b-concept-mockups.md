## DIRECTION A — *The Beastwardens* (Hogwarts-style academy + creatures in a hidden world)
Pitch: a kid who can hear magical creatures is taken to a hidden stronghold/academy where children train as Wardens guarding the border between our world and the wild magical lands. HP comfort (hidden society, chosen kid, school of wonder) + creatures front-and-center + an expansive world beyond the walls. Engine: each book = a new creature/threat/corner of the magical world; term/quest structure.

### Chapter One — The Thing Under the Clock Shop
Arlo Finch had lived above the clock shop for three years, and in all that time he had never once heard a clock lie. They ticked. They tocked. They told the truth about the hour whether you liked it or not.

So when he heard crying — thin and silver, coming from under the floor of the shop at midnight — he knew it wasn't a clock.

He crept down the narrow stairs in his socks. Three hundred clocks watched him from the walls, their pendulums swinging like a hundred slow heartbeats. Aunt Mildred was asleep. The crying came again, from behind the tall grandfather clock in the corner — the broken one, the one that had never worked and never sold.

Arlo pressed his ear to the wood. On the other side, something small was afraid.

"Hello?" he whispered. "Are you stuck?"

The crying stopped. A voice, no bigger than a thought, answered: *You can hear me?*

Arlo's skin prickled. "Course I can hear you. You're loud."

*No one hears us. Not in a hundred years.* The voice dropped lower. *You have to run. It's coming through, and when it comes, it will smell you.*

"When *what* comes—"

The grandfather clock struck. Not midnight — thirteen. A deep, wrong bell that Arlo felt in his teeth. And the wall behind it was gone, and where the wall had been there was a door of black wood, and the door was opening.

Cold poured out. Not winter cold — *old* cold, the cold of places that had never seen the sun. Through the gap Arlo saw something he could not make his eyes believe: a forest under a purple sky, trees taller than the town, and shapes moving between them that were too big and too quiet.

A hand caught his shoulder and spun him around.

A woman stood in the shop in a long grey coat, soaked with rain that hadn't fallen in town. She had a scar through one eyebrow and a sword that wasn't quite a sword — it hummed, like a struck glass. Behind her the clocks had all stopped at once.

"You heard the kit crying," she said. It wasn't a question. "Through a shut door. Through *dead* iron." She looked at him the way you look at a word you've been searching for all day. "What's your name, boy?"

"Arlo. Arlo Finch. I didn't— I didn't do anything—"

"No. You *are* something. That's worse, and better." She shoved a cloth bundle into his arms. It was warm, and it wriggled, and two enormous eyes blinked up at him out of a face like a fox made of smoke. "His name's Ash. He chose you, which means we don't have a choice either." The horn sounded then — a long, climbing note from beyond the black door, and the woman's face went hard as the floor. "That's the muster. Something's loose on the wrong side of the door."

She stepped through into the purple forest and held out her hand.

"You came on a bad night, Arlo Finch," she said. "Welcome to the Wardens. Try not to die in your first hour."

## DIRECTION B — *The Nine Lands* (LOTR-style quest across magical realms)
Pitch: the smallest, least likely child in a quiet village is the only one who can carry an ancient light across nine magical lands to keep a waking darkness asleep. Epic map, fellowship of companions from different folk, ancient history. Engine: each book = one new land + its peoples/creatures + a leg of the quest; series arc = the journey to the Sleeper.

### Chapter One — The Traveller Who Came at Dusk
In the village of Lowmarsh, nothing important had happened for two hundred years, and the people liked it that way.

Nima liked it less. She was small for eleven — small for nine, if she was honest — and in a village of broad, strong marsh-folk, being small meant being overlooked. She was good at being overlooked. She could sit so still the herons forgot she was there. She could cross the whole bog without the mud so much as sighing. "Light as a whisper, that one," the old women said, which was not a compliment in Lowmarsh.

It was the whisper that saved her life, in the end. But that came later.

That evening, Nima was the only one at the edge of the reeds when the traveller came out of the mist.

He should not have been able to. The marsh swallowed strangers — everyone knew that. But he walked the hidden paths as if someone had whispered them to him, and he came straight to Nima, and he was dying. She could see it. There was a wound under his cloak that glowed, faintly, the wrong colour.

"Lantern-born," he breathed, and fell to his knees in front of her. "At the last. I'd given up."

"I'll fetch the elder—" Nima started.

"No time. Listen." He pulled something from inside his cloak: a small lantern of grey metal, cold and dark. "Nine hundred years we kept the Sleeper sleeping. One light passed from hand to hand, land to land. The last Lantern-bearer is dead, and the Sleeper has begun to *dream*." His hand closed over hers around the cold lantern. "Only a hand light enough to walk unheard can carry it now. The Dark hears everything heavy. It will not hear *you*."

And then the lantern was not cold. A small flame woke inside it — pale gold, steady — and it had not been lit, and there was no wick, and Nima felt the warmth of it travel up her arm and into her chest like a swallowed star.

Far out in the marsh, something *noticed*. A sound rolled across the reeds that was not thunder, though the frogs went silent and the water shivered.

The traveller smiled, even as the light went out of his eyes. "Run, little whisper," he said. "Carry it to the Elves of the First Wood. They'll know the road." He slumped into the reeds, and was still.

Nima stood alone in the dusk, a dead man at her feet and a living light in her hands, and across nine lands she had never seen, the darkness opened one enormous eye.

## DIRECTION C — *The Beast Court* (mythic high-fantasy: hidden heir to a kingdom of creature-peoples)
Pitch: a kid who's always been "odd" (animals follow them) discovers they're the lost heir to a hidden kingdom of beast-folk and must claim an empty throne before a shadow does. Grand, mythic, "you are secretly important in a vast magical world" (HP chosen-one + LOTR kingdoms/peoples). Engine: each book = a new court/region/creature-people + a step toward securing the realm.

### Chapter One — The Boy the Animals Followed
Juno had always been followed by animals, and had always pretended not to notice.

It was easier that way. You could not explain why the crows walked you to school in a black, hopping crowd. You could not explain why the neighbour's vicious dog rolled over and showed its belly, or why, one terrible morning in Year Three, every cat on Mill Street had sat on the wall outside the window and *stared* until the teacher drew the curtains. You could not explain it, so Juno had learned to walk fast and keep their eyes down and be, as much as possible, nobody at all.

Mr. Quill changed that on a Tuesday.

He was waiting at the school gate — an old man in a coat the green of deep forests, leaning on a cane topped with a silver stag. The crows, Juno noticed, would not go near him. The crows were afraid.

"You have your mother's eyes," Mr. Quill said, as if they were halfway through a conversation. "Gold at the centre. She tried to hide it too."

Juno stopped. No one talked about their mother. There was nothing *to* talk about — a baby left on a doorstep, a note with one word, *Juno*, and eleven years of being the kid nobody claimed.

"I don't know you," Juno said.

"No. But the Court knows *you*, and so now, at last, does the thing that has been hunting the Court's blood for eleven years." The old man's eyes flicked to the sky, and his voice dropped. "It found your scent this morning. That is why I am early, and why we are already late."

Overhead, the afternoon went wrong. The light dimmed, though there were no clouds. The crows burst off the wall in a screaming spiral, and the temperature dropped so fast Juno's breath turned to fog. Down the empty street, between the parked cars, a *shadow* was pooling where no one stood to cast it — and it was growing, and it had the shape of a great hound made of nothing at all.

Mr. Quill's cane split open. The silver stag at its top was suddenly a stag of light, huge and antlered, lowering its head between Juno and the dark.

"The Beast Court has been without a ruler for eleven years, child," the old man said, "and an empty throne draws wolves. You are the heir. You are the *only* heir." He seized Juno's hand, and the whole world bent sideways — the street folding like paper, a green and golden country unfolding behind it, full of wings and horns and ten thousand waiting eyes. "Run *now*, Your Majesty. Explanations later. Survival first."
