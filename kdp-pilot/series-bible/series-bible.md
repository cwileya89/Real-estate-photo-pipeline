## 1. Logline
Ten-year-old Bram Flint can hear things no one else can — including a creature crying through solid iron. That gift pulls him through a door behind a broken clock into **the Wild**, a vast world of beasts and beast-folk, and into the **Beastwardens**: the ancient order that guards the border between the two worlds — just as that border begins to come apart.

## 2. Series premise
The human world (**the Tame**) and the magical world (**the Wild**) were once one, and it was ruin for both. Long ago the first Wardens raised **the Veil** — an unseen border — and split the world in two, keeping the doors between them and tending the balance ever since. Now the Veil is **thinning**: doors open on their own, dangerous beasts slip into the Tame, and something is unmaking the border from the far side. The Beastwardens train rare children who can sense beasts to hold the line. Bram is the strongest such child in a generation — and the thinning seems to be following him.

**Why it's the format the owner wants, at a scale that ships:** the Wild is a huge world of many lands and many creature-peoples (the Harry-Potter-meets-Lord-of-the-Rings feel). Each book opens a **new region of the Wild + a new beast-people + a new threat** (expansive world across the series), while every volume stays ~12k words and self-contained. The series arc — *who is thinning the Veil, and why* — pulls readers book to book for KU read-through.

## 3. Protagonist — Bram Flint
- **Age 10. Boy.** (Swappable to a girl; see §11. Boy chosen to match the mock the owner picked and to counter the girl-skew of the fae space.)
- Orphan-ish: lives above **Aunt Mildred's clock shop** in the small town of **Draybeck**. His parents are "gone"; the hidden truth (series legacy thread, revealed across the arc) is that **his mother was a Warden** who vanished investigating the thinning. Explains his gift and ties him to the central mystery.
- **Gift:** he *hears* beasts — and he hears them through **dead iron**, which should be impossible (iron deadens magic). Raised among 300 clocks, he knows when a sound "rings false." His tool is **listening and cleverness, not power** — he out-thinks problems, which keeps him an active, relatable hero and models attentiveness for the reader.
- **Companion:** **Ash**, a **smoke-fox** kit who chose him the first night — loyal, mischievous, made of drifting grey smoke with two huge eyes. Bram's anchor and the **series cover mascot**.
- **Arc:** outsider "iron boy" on trial → trusted fledgling Warden with a purpose (find out what happened to his mother; hold the Veil).

## 4. World — the Tame, the Wild, and Wardenhold
**Rules of magic (keep consistent across all books):**
1. **Two worlds, one Veil.** The Veil is the unseen border; **doors** (thresholds in old, forgotten places — behind a broken clock, under a bridge, inside a hollow tree) are the only crossings. Most are shut and watched.
2. **Iron deadens magic.** Cities of iron and clocks are why the Tame forgot the Wild. Bram hearing *through* iron marks his gift as extraordinary.
3. **The gift & bonding is rare** — not everyone gets a beast; most humans have no gift at all. A bonded companion chooses its Warden; it is a partnership, not a pet. *(This is the key differentiator from "bond-a-beast" series like Spirit Animals / Wilderlore: Wardens are border-guardians first.)*
4. **Wild magic runs on instinct, season, and territory** — beasts can be reasoned with, bargained with, or provoked; they are peoples and forces, not monsters-of-the-week.
5. **The thinning** is the series engine: the Veil is weakening, doors open unbidden, and the balance is failing. Safe-for-age stakes: menace and wonder, never gore.

**The Wild (revealed one region per book — the "magical lands" payoff):**
- **The Threshold Wood** — the purple-skied forest just past the doors, patrolled by Wardens (Book 1).
- **Wardenhold** — the great border-keep: half-fortress, half-menagerie, built around the oldest and largest door. Home and academy of the Wardens; where fledglings train (beast-lore, tracking, door-craft, healing). *This is the "Hogwarts" — belonging, mentors, rival kids, wonder.*
- **The Ashfell Mountains** — fire-drake country; doors failing fastest (Book 2 expedition).
- **The Glass Fens**, **the Thunderwood**, **the Singing Marsh**, **the Underdeep** — later regions, each with its own beast-people.

**The threat (series antagonist):**
- **The Shrike** — a cloaked figure opening doors from the inside; believes the Veil *should* fall and the two worlds rejoin. A graspable, human-scale villain (the "Voldemort" the age group needs), with an understandable-but-dangerous conviction. Tied to Bram's mother's disappearance.
- **The Unmade** — the ancient danger the Veil was raised to cage, sealed beyond the last door, stirring as the thinning spreads. The looming dread behind the Shrike. Revealed slowly; confronted in Book 3.

## 5. Recurring cast (locked art designs)
- **Ash** — smoke-fox companion; mascot; on every cover.
- **Warden Vane** — Bram's gruff mentor: grey coat, scar through one eyebrow, a humming glass "sword" that is really a tuned blade of Wild-glass. Recruited him; believes in him last.
- **Nettle** — a fellow fledgling, Bram's first friend at the Keep: quick, loyal, knows every passage, grew up in Wardenhold. (The trio warmth.)
- **Warden-Keeper Rook** — ancient head of the order; wise, weary, keeper of the history and of secrets about Bram's mother. (The Dumbledore seat; appears sparingly.)
- **Corvin** — a sneering rival fledgling who calls Bram "the iron boy." Foil, not a true villain.
- **Spotlight beast/region per book** (≤2 new designs/book — the ceiling discipline): Book 1 — the loose shadow-beast (a "gaunt-hound") + the smoke-foxes. Later books — fire-drakes, fen-folk, etc.

## 6. Tone & style guide
- **Tone:** immersive wonder + real adventure + warmth. Bigger, moodier, more epic than a cozy chapter book — lantern-lit keeps, purple skies, ancient stakes — but anchored by friendship and belonging.
- **Reading level target:** grades 3–5. Short-to-medium sentences, concrete vivid imagery, dialogue-driven; introduce world-vocabulary (Veil, the Wild, fledgling, the thinning) with context on first use. Chapters ~900–1,000 words.
- **Chapter craft:** every chapter ends on a pull-forward beat; the final chapter hooks the next book (an expedition into a new land of the Wild). Chapter 1 must convert the free Kindle sample — it opens on the mock the owner approved.
- **KDP children's-content guardrails (hard rules):** menace and "safe scares" only — no gore, no on-page death of a child, no real-world weapons used on people, no romance, no profanity. Peril is thrilling but bloodless; beasts are driven back or outwitted, not slaughtered. Values: courage, listening, loyalty, protecting others. Nothing that trips Amazon's children's guidelines or needs a content warning.

## 7. Three-book arc (throughline: *the Veil is thinning — who is doing it, and why*)
- **Book 1 — *The Door Behind the Clock*:** Bram is found, brought to Wardenhold, trains as a fledgling, and in stopping a beast loose in the Tame discovers the breach was *made*, not natural — the Veil is thinning on purpose, and the Shrike is behind it. He earns his place; learns his mother was a Warden who vanished. *Self-contained win; series wound opened.*
- **Book 2 — *The Ashfell Doors* (manuscript later):** an expedition into the Wild — the fire-drake mountains, where doors fail fastest. First great "magical lands" set-piece and a new beast-people. A betrayal reveals the Shrike has allies inside the order; Bram finds the first real trace of his mother. *Raises stakes; widens the world.*
- **Book 3 — *The Last Door* (outline only this pilot):** race to stop the Shrike from unsealing the Unmade; Bram's rare gift and his mother's lost knowledge are the key. A bittersweet, heroic close — the Veil held, at a cost — that keeps the series open. 

## 8. Art bible seed (feeds V5 — full lock happens there)
- **Locked recurring designs (reused every book):** Bram (clock-shop boy, patched coat, listening tilt of the head), Ash (smoke-fox), Warden Vane (grey coat, scar, glass-blade), Nettle, the **Wardenhold gate**.
- **Per-book NEW-design budget: ≤ 2 new beasts/regions.** Everything else is the recurring cast + reused settings. *(Ceiling discipline — more recurring characters than a cozy concept, so new-design spend per book is capped hard.)*
- **Style direction:** atmospheric storybook — confident ink linework + moody painterly color; lantern-gold light against deep blues/purples; awe + a little danger; cozy only at the Keep. Must read as a grayscale spot at 6×9 **and** as a thumbnail.
- **Palette:** deep twilight blue/violet, iron-grey, lantern gold, smoke-silver (Ash), ember-orange accents.
- **Hard constraints:** 300 DPI at 6×9 · print-safe (test in grayscale, no color-only reliance) · **no imitation of any named living illustrator** · consistent turnaround via locked character sheet.

## 9. Competitive honesty (flagged per brief)
The "kids + magical creatures + hidden world" shelf is **crowded**: Wilderlore, Spirit Animals, The Menagerie, Beast Quest, Beastopia (ages 7–9), Wings of Fire. This validates demand **and** is real competition for a no-review debut. **Our edges:** (1) the **Warden/border-guardian** frame (guarding two worlds) rather than generic beast-bonding; (2) **illustrated + short + KU-priced** squarely at ages 7–10, where Wings of Fire (longer/older) and Beast Quest (older brand identity) leave a gap; (3) the distinctive **clock/iron / "hears through dead iron"** hook. Discoverability still depends on cover + sample + reviews (V1) — the concept doesn't remove that cold-start risk.
