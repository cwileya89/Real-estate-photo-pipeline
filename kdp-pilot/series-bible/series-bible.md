## 1. Logline
When nine-year-old Birdie moves to the edge of an old wood, she finds a door inside a tree — and steps into the Hollow, a hidden world of elves, pixies, hobs and wilder folk. She becomes its first **Hollow-friend** in a generation, just as the doors between the two worlds begin to close for good.

## 2. Series premise
Folded invisibly into the ordinary human world is **the Hollow**: a vast, wild realm of fae folk, each kind with its own magic. Humans forgot it long ago, and as they forgot, the **thresholds** (doors in trees, gaps in hedges, the dark under a bridge) began to close and the Hollow's magic began to fade. A Hollow-friend is the rare human who can cross between worlds and tend that border — and there hasn't been one for a generation. Birdie is the next. Each book, she opens a new corner of the Hollow, meets new folk with new abilities, and faces one more sign that something is pulling the Hollow's magic away — while making the first real friends of her life on the other side of the tree.

**The engine (why it scales cheaply):** every book = one new realm + one new folk + one new ability + a keepsake "token" Birdie earns (a collectible/completion hook, à la Rainbow Magic). The world grows across the series; each volume stays short and is self-contained with a clear problem, while the "closing doors" arc pulls readers book-to-book for KU read-through.

## 3. Protagonist — Birdie Hazelwood
- **Age 9. Girl.** (See §11 — easily swappable to a boy if the owner wants to skew readership; market note there.)
- Recently moved to the town of **Thornhollow** to live with her **Gran** for a year while her mum works far away. Lonely, the new kid, no friends yet — the outsider hook that converts the free sample (V1).
- **Traits:** notices everything (sharp eyes, asks "why"), brave in a quiet way, kind to small overlooked things, stubborn. Her human-world cleverness (not magic) is what repeatedly saves the day — she solves fae problems with observation and nerve. This keeps her the active hero and models problem-solving for the reader.
- **Want:** to belong somewhere. **Arc across series:** from lonely outsider → trusted Hollow-friend who bridges two worlds and, by Book 3, chooses the harder good over the easy one.
- **Voice/aesthetic:** practical, a little wry, wears her late grandad's too-big raincoat with deep pockets. Deliberately **not** sparkly/pink — the Hollow is wild and a touch dangerous, to pull boys as well as girls.

## 4. World — the Hollow
**Rules of magic (keep consistent across all books):**
1. The Hollow is reached through **thresholds** — ordinary-looking gaps (a door in a tree, a hedge-hole, under a bridge) that open to a Hollow-friend, or to anyone on the old crossing-days. Most are closed or closing.
2. Hollow magic is **wild magic** — tied to seasons, weather, growing things, and *memory*. It can't be commanded like a machine; it's coaxed, bargained with, or borrowed (and borrowing has a cost — the source of Book 1's trouble).
3. Only a **Hollow-friend** can touch fading/broken magic without being harmed, and can carry a token between worlds. That's why Birdie matters.
4. **Iron, clocks, and forgetting** weaken the Hollow. The more the human world paves, hurries, and forgets the wild, the faster the doors close. *(This is the light stewardship thread — present as texture, never a lecture. See §11 to confirm.)*
5. **No clocks run true in the Hollow.** Time is loose but not a trap — Birdie always gets home by supper, which keeps stakes safe for the age group.

**Geography (revealed one region per book — the expansive-world payoff):**
- **The Bramblewood** — elf country, green and tangled (Bramble's home).
- **Underhill** — warren of the hobs and brownies; old, cozy, grumpy.
- **The Pixie Night-Market** — a traveling market that only appears after dark; pixies trade in riddles, favors, and small magics (Book 2 hub).
- **The Frostfell** — the cold edge of the Hollow where winter is kept (Book 1 climax).
- **The Glimmermarsh** — will-o'-the-wisp wetlands, beautiful and treacherous (later).
- **The Weft** — not a place but a **living map** of every realm, woven of light; most of it has gone dark as realms fall silent. Birdie is shown it in Book 1; its darkening is the series' visible ticking clock.

**The threat (series antagonist — a force, not a cackling villain):** **the Fading** (in-world some folk call it "the Hush") — the slow draining of the Hollow's magic as the human world forgets the wild. In Book 2 it has a visible agent (a magic-thief with an understandable, not evil, motive); in Book 3 its true cause ties to Gran's past. Age-appropriate: dread and loss, never gore or true villainy.

## 5. Recurring cast
- **Bramble** — a young elf, Birdie's age in elf-years; leaf-cloaked, anxious, big-hearted, talks too fast when nervous. His well-meant spell starts Book 1. Birdie's first real friend and guide; **the series' cover mascot** (appears on every cover with Birdie). *Locked art design.*
- **Old Thistle** — an ancient hob, keeper of Hollow-lore, grumpy and suspicious of humans, secretly kind. Names Birdie a Hollow-friend; the mentor who doles out world-rules and warnings. *Locked art design; not on every cover.*
- **Gran (Enid Hazelwood)** — Birdie's grandmother in the human world; warm, unflappable, makes a lot of tea. Drops cryptic lines that pay off in Book 3: **Gran was the last Hollow-friend.** Human-world anchor and emotional core.
- **Spotlight folk (one new per book, each a new locked design that book only):** Book 1 — the frost that leaks (environmental, plus the pixies at the toll bridge). Book 2 — the magic-thief + the Night-Market pixies. Book 3 — a will-o'-the-wisp / the wild-magic being.

## 6. Tone & style guide
- **Tone:** warm wonder + real adventure + gentle humor. Cozy enough for bedtime, with genuine stakes and quiet emotional beats (loneliness → belonging).
- **Reading level target:** grades 3–5. Short sentences, concrete vocabulary, heavy on dialogue and action. Introduce "magic" vocabulary (threshold, wild magic, Hollow-friend) with context on first use. Chapters ~800–950 words. *(Per-chapter reading level is measured and flagged in V4.)*
- **Chapter craft:** every chapter ends on a pull-forward beat (question, reveal, or rising danger); the final chapter hooks the next book. Open Book 1 Chapter 1 strong enough to convert the free Kindle sample.
- **KDP children's-content guardrails (hard rules):** mild jeopardy only (no gore, no death-of-child peril, no real-world weapons used on people); no romance; no profanity; frightening-but-safe ("safe scares"); kindness, courage, and curiosity as values; nothing that would trip Amazon's children's guidelines or need a content warning.

## 7. Three-book arc (throughline: *the doors are closing*)
- **Book 1 — *The Door in the Tree*:** Birdie finds the Hollow, befriends Bramble, undoes his leaking-winter spell by returning the "borrowed winter" to the Frostfell, and is named Hollow-friend. She learns the thresholds have been closing for a generation and sees the first sign that the magic is being *pulled* away. *Self-contained win; series wound opened.*
- **Book 2 — *The Night-Market* (outline/draft later):** Magic is being stolen from the folk; Birdie and Bramble track the thief through the Pixie Night-Market into deeper realms. Twist: the "thief" isn't evil — it's a desperate folk trying to survive the Fading, and the real cause reaches toward the human world. *Complicates the arc; raises the stakes.*
- **Book 3 — *The Last Door* (outline only this pilot):** The thresholds begin sealing for good. To keep even one door open, Birdie must wake the Hollow's oldest **wild magic** — and the secret is Gran's: she was the last Hollow-friend, and she closed a door once to protect someone. Birdie makes a bittersweet choice (gives up easy crossing) to keep one door open. *Satisfying trilogy close; series stays open for more.*

## 8. Art bible seed (feeds V5 — full lock happens there)
- **Locked recurring designs (reused every book):** Birdie (raincoat, deep pockets, sharp eyes), Bramble (leaf-cloak, mascot), Old Thistle (hob). Recurring hub: **the hawthorn threshold tree**.
- **Per-book NEW-design budget: ≤ 2 new folk/creatures** (ceiling discipline). Everything else is Birdie + Bramble + reused settings in new arrangements.
- **Style direction:** warm contemporary storybook — clean confident linework + soft painterly color; cozy-but-wild, not cutesy, not pink. Readable as a small B&W/grayscale spot at 6×9 **and** as a thumbnail.
- **Palette:** mossy greens, twilight blues, frost-silver, warm lantern-gold accents.
- **Hard constraints:** 300 DPI at 6×9 trim · print-safe (no color-only reliance; test in grayscale) · **no imitation of any named living illustrator** · consistent character turnaround via a locked character sheet.
